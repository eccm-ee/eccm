Body sans

A body face for long text and tables, sturdier and narrower than Instrument Sans. Every paragraph is set at today’s measure, so more letters a line is a narrower face and a darker grey a sturdier one.

Today’s measure is 66 ch, and a ch is the width of the face’s zero: Instrument Sans draws a wide zero, so the site’s lines run about 98 letters, past the 75 the type rules allow. The last column says what 66 ch would give in each face.

  1. Source Sans 3: the narrowest and the darkest at 400, a smaller x-height, and figures tabular by default.
  2. Hanken Grotesk: Inter’s qualities in a less familiar face, darker and lower than Instrument Sans, tabular by default.
  3. Atkinson Hyperlegible Next: every letter distinct, dark and compact, though its slashed zero reads technical in prose.

x-height as a share of the size. Letters a line at the site’s measure, and at 66 ch of the face’s own zero. Stem: the l’s stroke in thousandths of the size. Colour: the stem over the average letter’s width, higher is darker. Ink: the share of a line’s box the paragraph covers at 18 px. Figures: tabular by default, or through tnum.

All three sit under the current Instrument Sans headings as a body face alone; Source Sans 3 and Hanken Grotesk also carry the headings at 400 if the whole site changes.

Left out: Inter Tight, which is Inter; Geist, now as common as Inter; Figtree, Manrope and Albert Sans, geometric and wide at text size; Work Sans, wide and display at its ends; Overpass, a signage voice; Commissioner, whose flair axis is for headings; Hubot Sans, Mona Sans’s display partner. Instrument Sans at 450 and Schibsted Grotesk are measured in the table only: the first is darker and just as wide, the second widens . and : in tabular figures.

A. Instrument Sans, now

The body face today, at 400, its lightest weight. Its stem is thin for its width, so the page reads grey, and its letters are as big as Inter’s.

The Estonian Centre of Contemporary Music brings six evenings of new music to Tallinn this autumn, each built around a composer working with a performer. From 214 proposals the jury chose pieces that will be heard for the first time and have never been recorded. Concerts begin at 19.30 in the hall at Pühavaimu 9; tickets are 12 euros, 8 for students and pensioners. A Swiss ensemble shares the stage with young players from Tartu, and the late conversations continue in the foyer after each performance. Details, the programme and notes on the performers are on each event’s page, with a map and access information.
